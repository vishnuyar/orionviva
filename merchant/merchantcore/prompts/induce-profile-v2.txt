You are given transaction descriptions printed by ONE bank on ONE kind of
statement. A bank composes these lines from a small number of fixed templates,
filling holes with details that change per transaction. Your task is to recover
those templates.

Return the templates and nothing else. Do not identify merchants, do not
categorise anything, do not comment on any individual transaction.

A template is literal text plus named holes in curly braces:

    ZELLE TO {{counterparty}} {{reference}}
    CARD PURCHASE {{date}} {{brand}} {{city}} {{region}}

RULES, all of them strict:

1. A hole may ONLY be one of these names. Nothing else is accepted, and a
   template using any other name is discarded:

{slots}

2. You may not write regular expressions, character classes, wildcards,
   quantifiers, or alternation. Only literal words and named holes. Each hole
   already knows what shape of text it matches.

3. A template must describe the WHOLE line from beginning to end. Every part of
   a line must fall either inside a hole or inside the literal text. If part of
   a line is filler that nothing needs, put it in {{noise}}.

4. Generalise — with one exception, and the exception is not rare.

   Usually a template that reproduces one example exactly, with no holes, is an
   example rather than a grammar. Write the shape that would match every line of
   that kind, including ones not shown here.

   THE EXCEPTION is a line with nothing variable in it. A fee, a payment
   acknowledgement, an interest charge, a credit: the bank prints the identical
   string every time, and that sameness is what the line IS. Write those as
   literal text with no holes at all. They are templates, not examples.

   Two things not to do when a line looks like that. Do not invent a hole to
   satisfy the rule about generalising — an invented hole matches text that is
   never there. And do not shorten the line to make it look more general: a
   template must still describe the line from beginning to end, so a truncated
   version of a fixed phrase matches nothing at all and is worse than either.

5. A hole name may appear AT MOST ONCE in a template. Some lines carry two
   different identifiers — a transfer id and a transaction number, an
   originator's id and a trace number. Use two different names for them:
   {{reference}}, {{trace}} and {{company_id}} are three distinct holes and
   exist precisely for this. Repeating one name discards the template.

6. Prefer FEW templates. If two lines differ only in the value of a hole, they
   are one template. If they differ in their literal words, they are two.

7. Order matters: the first template that matches a line wins, so put more
   specific templates before more general ones.

8. THE PERSON. A payment between two people identifies the other one however the
   sender addressed them, and all of these are the same thing:

       a name          ZELLE TO {{counterparty}} {{reference}}
       a phone number  ZELLE TO {{counterparty_handle}} {{reference}}
       an email        ZELLE TO {{counterparty_handle}} {{reference}}
       a username      ZELLE TO {{counterparty_handle}} {{reference}}

   Use {{counterparty}} when the hole holds a person's NAME. Use
   {{counterparty_handle}} when it holds a phone number, an email address or a
   username standing in for a person. Never use {{contact}} for either —
   {{contact}} is a phone number or web address a MERCHANT printed, which is
   public, and these are not.

   A shop, a company, a utility or a bank is {{brand}} or {{institution}}, never
   a counterparty of any kind. This distinction decides what is treated as
   private, so err toward the counterparty holes when a hole clearly holds a
   person and away from them for anything else.

9. If some lines do not fit any template you can write, leave them out. A
   grammar that honestly covers most lines is worth more than one that claims
   all of them by being vague.

{{"templates": ["...", "..."]}}
