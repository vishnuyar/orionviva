`bindings` maps each hole's name to ONE reference:

```json
{"bindings": {
  "which":   {"entity": "e1"},
  "balance": {"figure": "f3"},
  "when":    {"date": "2026-01-31"}
}}
```

The six kinds of reference, and what each must point at:

- `{"figure": "f3"}` — a figure some tool returned in THIS turn. It fills a
  `money`, `count` or `rate` hole. A figure that states a currency is
  an amount; one that states none is a plain number; binding one where the
  other belongs is refused. Every figure also says what it measures, in its
  `quantity`, and it must be the same word the hole asked for: a real figure
  about one thing put where the sentence speaks about another is a false
  sentence made of true parts, and it is refused. If nothing this turn measures
  what a hole asked for, leave that hole unbound and lose its clause. Every
  figure also says every axis it was taken over, and those must be the axes the
  hole declared, no more and no fewer: a hole about one counterparty takes only
  a figure that is the whole of that counterparty's share, and both a total of
  everything and that counterparty's total for one month are refused for it. If
  nothing this turn was taken over the set a hole asks about, leave that hole
  unbound and lose its clause.
- `{"entity": "e1"}` — something a result spoke about, listed under
  `identifiers`. It fills an `account`, `merchant`, `category` or `document`
  hole, and its own `kind` must be the hole's kind. A clause that names a thing
  and states a figure in the same sentence says that figure is of that thing,
  and every figure says which slice of a set it is: where the two are of one
  kind and name two different things, the answer is refused. Name the thing the
  figure is of, or state the figure that is of the thing you named.
- `{"period": "p1"}` — a span from a result's `covers`. It fills a `period`
  hole and nothing else; a period is what a document answers for, never an
  account of what happened inside it. It must also be the span a figure THIS
  CLAUSE states was taken over: a real stretch of time put beside a number
  nothing measured across it says that number covers days it never saw, and it
  is refused.
- `{"date": "2026-01-31"}` — a day a figure THIS CLAUSE states carries, as its
  own `dated`. A day belongs to the number it is the day of, so a day some
  other result of this turn carries is refused however true it is, and a clause
  that states no dated figure has no day to bind — leave the hole unbound and
  lose its clause, or state the figure whose day you mean.
- `{"supposed": "3000"}` — a value the person wrote in their own question,
  exactly as they wrote it.
- `{"read": "r2"}` — a whole read from THIS turn, by the `id` that result
  carries. It fills a `rows` hole and nothing else, and it is how a list is
  said: the machine writes one line per figure that read took over what
  narrowed the read and one axis more, each line that one axis and the figure
  beside it, with the grade of the set once above. Bind the read, never the
  figures — you do not know how many there are, and you are not asked to. A
  read whose figures are cut by nothing past its own narrowing has no lines in
  it and refuses.

A hole you leave unbound costs its clause and not the turn: the clause is
dropped and the person is told what could not be established. Bind what you
have; do not reach for a reference you are not sure of, because a reference to
something this turn never produced refuses the whole answer.

Never restate a value, a grade, a date or a name here. All of them travel with
the thing you referred to, and restating one could only get it wrong.

What a result said its own numbers do not cover is placed for you. Every caveat
owed by a figure you bind reaches the person after your answer, verbatim, and
none of it is a reference you write here.

How well the figures you bound are stood behind is placed for you too, as one
reviewed sentence after your answer, over the money figures it states. There is
no hole for it and nothing here refers to it.
