# tool: query_ledger
Read the ledger in totals. This is the tool that answers "how much", and for most questions one call of it is the whole answer. entity says what to read: "balances" (each real account's balance, with grade and source), "transactions" (a summary of the movements matching the filters — how many, how many months they span, money in, money out, net, and the same broken down by account and by month; it returns no individual rows, and the month count is the divisor a per-month average is computed with), "holdings" (measured positions from each account's latest statement), "aggregate" (metric "spending" grouped by group_by, metric "income" per currency, or metric "net_worth" as of a date), or "vocabulary" (the labels this vault holds under the vocabulary group_by names, and how many).
net_worth is the direct answer to "what am I worth": it takes no filters, it returns net, assets and liabilities per currency and a line per account, and it is complete on its own — never assemble a net worth yourself out of balances and holdings.
income is a lifetime figure over everything ingested; it does not answer by date window yet, and it says so in its own caveats.
Counterparties are matched on an exact key, never on a name you guess. "vocabulary" with group_by "merchant" and a "matching" argument is how you find the key behind a name the person used; put one of the keys it hands back in a merchant filter. A key is what a filter takes and an id is what a binding refers to: they are different fields of the same thing, and neither is accepted where the other belongs.
filters narrow by account, category, tag, merchant, currency, or a from/to date window; every filter value must be one the vault actually holds, and an unknown value is refused with the known values named. A filter an entity does not honour is refused rather than ignored.
A read answers for a day that has happened. `as_of` may name today or any day before it — a balance carries forward, so absent a newer statement the last one observed is still what the account holds, and with no `as_of` a total is good as of today. A day still to come refuses: carrying a balance forward is not projecting one.

vocabulary is the direct answer to "how many X do I have" and "which X do I have". It takes no filters and needs a group_by: category, subcategory, tag, merchant, account or currency. It also takes an optional "matching", a name to look for, which decides which labels come back and nothing else: first the ones whose key is that name, then the ones whose key begins with it, then the ones carrying it as a whole word, in that order. The count is the whole vocabulary's size either way, matched or not, and every label that comes back is a value a filter takes. This is the one place a name is read generously — past it a filter value is exact, so look a name up here and narrow on what you were handed. "matching" belongs to this entity and is refused on the others. Its count is a different number from the number of groups a spending breakdown returns, and the two are not interchangeable — a breakdown counts the labels this person's SPENDING falls into, which leaves out a label used only on income, one whose movements are all transfers, and one named in a ruling with nothing posted against it yet. Ask this when the question is about the vocabulary; ask for a breakdown when it is about the money. Both count in the same unit: a subcategory is named under the category it slices, in a vocabulary read exactly as in a breakdown, because two categories may each hold a "fees" and a bare label is therefore not one thing.

Grouping spending by subcategory keys each group by its category and its own name — `groceries / supermarket`, and `groceries / unassigned` for money that category holds which no subcategory names. That pair is a group key and not a category: no `identifiers` entry is returned for it, and it is not a value the category filter accepts. What it can be spoken through is the figure it is attached to — under every grouping, each group's figure carries the slice it was taken over, which is what a block of rows writes beside it — but that is the scope of a number, never a name to ask a follow-up with. Group by category when the answer needs to name what it is about.

Every number returned comes back as a figure with an id, its grade and the documents it rests on. Every figure also says what it measures, and an account someone owes on — a card, a loan — is measured as what is owed rather than as what is held: its figure comes back as "owed", written as the bill prints it, positive where the person owes and negative where the account owes them. What is held comes back as "balance", and the two are different kinds of thing: neither can be spoken as the other, and adding one to the other refuses. In a net worth, what is owed and what is held are the same two kinds again, which is why the direct read of net worth is the only thing that puts them together. Amounts are exact decimal strings; nothing is converted between currencies. Every amount states the currency it is in, and a count — of movements, accounts, holdings or documents — states none, because it is a plain number rather than an amount of anything. A window in which nothing moved still answers with amounts: a total of zero in the currency of the accounts it ranged over, resting on them. A summary whose movements are not all in one currency refuses rather than adding across them; narrow it with the currency filter and ask once per currency.
Everything a read speaks about, and not only what it measures, comes back with an identity. `identifiers` lists the accounts, counterparties and categories the read spoke about, each an object with its own id and kind; when you bind a clause of your answer, refer to one of those ids rather than writing a name. A filter is the other case and takes no id: it takes the value the entity carries beside its id — an account its path, a category its label, a counterparty its key. `covers` lists the spans the read is attested for, one per account, and `caveats` are the read's own sentences about what its numbers do not cover.

# tool: list_movements
The individual movements, as rows. This and the transactions summary read the same movements and differ in what comes back: the summary returns how much moved and no rows, and this returns the rows themselves. A question about which purchases were made — what was bought somewhere, which payments went out, what carries a tag — is asking for rows. filters is required, and inside it at least one filter narrow enough to name what the question is about: account, category, merchant, tag or window. currency is honoured and is not one of those, so a call narrowed by currency alone is refused as too broad — a ledger is mostly in one currency, and naming it names no question. It shows at most a page of rows, stating how many matched in total and how to narrow further. Each row carries its record id, date, description, effect, currency, nature, category, tags, grade and source document; each effect comes back as a figure, and each counterparty as an entity you can refer to. `effect` is how much moved and which way: negative is money out of your life, positive is money in. It is read off the kind of account the movement sits on, never off the raw sign a statement printed — a purchase on a card is recorded as what you owe growing, and comes back negative because the money is gone. The raw signed posting is not a field you are given, in this read or in get_provenance, so there is no second number to reconcile against this one.

# tool: check_completeness
How complete and how current the picture is: documents held versus posted, statements awaiting review and why, each account's latest evidenced date, and how many counterparties have no category yet. Ask before trusting a total for anything that needs every statement present, and use the answer to say what a figure does not yet include. It is not a prerequisite for every question — a read that already carries its own coverage and caveats has told you what it does not cover.

# tool: get_provenance
Where a figure comes from. Give record_id as a movement key, an account id, or a document id; the answer names the source document, page and region, the verification grade, and — for a movement — the rung that decided its nature. Use it to answer "why do you say that?" about any figure another tool returned.

# tool: get_transparency
What the agent has done on its own and what questions were set aside. topic "agent_activity" lists every unattended action with its outcome; "calls_spent" counts the model calls the agent spent, optionally since a date; "declined_questions" lists the questions the person set aside and the stake each showed when declined. Its figures are about the agent's own behaviour and cite the ledger events that recorded it, not documents.

# tool: compute
Exact decimal arithmetic over figures this turn already produced. expression uses +, -, *, /, parentheses, integer literals and names. inputs binds each name to either the id of a figure some tool returned in this turn, or an object {"stipulated": "<the amount the person named in their question>"} for a value they supposed rather than one on record. A decimal typed in directly is refused: if a number is worth computing with, some tool emitted it and it has an id.
Every operand is one of two kinds of quantity, and the operators only combine them in the ways that mean something. An amount of money is a figure that states a currency, and a value the person supposed. A plain number is an integer literal you wrote, and any figure that states no currency — a count of movements, of accounts, of holdings, of documents.
Money adds to and subtracts from money in the same currency, and the result is money. Money multiplied or divided by a plain number is money: that is how you annualise, halve, or split by a number of people or months. Two amounts divided by each other give a plain number — a ratio, how many times over — and that is how you compare a balance with a total or express a proportion. Two plain numbers give a plain number.
The rest refuses. Adding a plain number to an amount refuses, so a magnitude you type beside a figure cannot pass as money. Multiplying two amounts refuses, because there is nothing money-squared measures. Dividing a plain number by an amount refuses. So does mixing a figure about my own behaviour with one about your money, and so do two currencies the same expression reaches — a figure you bind but never name in the expression decides nothing about it.
The result is itself a figure of kind "computed", and it answers two separate questions about itself.
What it rests on is the first. Multiplying or dividing by a magnitude you wrote changes the units and leaves the evidence where it was, so the result stands on the documents of every operand that has any, at the weakest grade among those that carry one — a count with a grade of its own contributes it rather than being dropped. Adding or subtracting a magnitude you wrote puts a quantity nothing measured into the total instead, so a total counts as evidenced only when every term is: if any term stands on no document, the result stands on none and carries no grade, and being a claim about money it cannot be said at all. Ask for the thing you actually want computed rather than for an expression that reaches a number by adding one in.
How the arithmetic came out is the second, and it is not a matter of evidence. A result that cannot be written exactly is not refused: it comes back rounded and its "exactness" field says "rounded", while a figure with no "exactness" field came out exactly. An amount of money is rounded to the nearest hundredth, and a plain number to a fixed count of significant figures, so a proportion far smaller than a hundredth still comes back as itself rather than as zero. Its grade and its documents are unaffected — a weekly or monthly share of a corroborated amount is still corroborated. Write your sentence about such a figure plainly, with no hedge of your own: the approximation is recorded on the figure and said in the answer for you.
A result is marked "hypothetical" rather than "computed" if anything the person supposed reached it, directly or through an earlier computed figure, and a hypothetical carries no grade. Never do arithmetic yourself; pass it here.
